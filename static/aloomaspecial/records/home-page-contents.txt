Why Does the Same Drain Keep Clogging After It Has Been Cleaned?<h2>
A clogged drain is rarely just a clogged drain. Most recurring drain problems have a structural or mechanical explanation that a single snaking visit does not address.
Grease does not flush out. It sticks to pipe walls, cools, and hardens in layers. Each time the drain runs, a thin film deposits on the layer before it. A basic cable snake cuts a hole through the obstruction. The grease lining the walls stays intact. Within weeks, the hole closes again.
Root intrusion works the same way. Fine root threads push through pipe joints seeking moisture. A snake breaks the threads at the entry point, but the root mass outside the pipe stays. New threads push back in within weeks.
Hydrojetting uses water pressure, typically between 3,000 and 4,000 PSI depending on pipe material, to scour the pipe wall in all directions as the hose moves through the line. That removes the wall buildup, not just the center obstruction. The difference is between a temporary fix and a clean pipe.

When Cleaning Is Not the Right Answer at All<h2>
Sometimes a recurring drain problem is not a cleaning problem. It is a structural one. Collapsed pipe segments, offset joints, or root damage severe enough to have fractured the pipe wall require a different approach entirely. That is why camera inspection matters before any significant drain cleaning work. You need to see what is in the line before deciding what to do about it.

How do I know the technician will recommend what I actually need and not just the most expensive option? The diagnostic process is transparent by design. The goal is a recommendation you would agree with if you had the same information we do.^^
What is the difference between a drain line problem and a sewer line problem? A drain line serves a single fixture or a small section of your home, such as a bathroom or kitchen. The main sewer line, also called the sewer lateral, is the single pipe that collects waste from every drain in the house and carries it to the municipal connection or a private septic system. A clog in a single drain line affects one area. A clog or damage in the main sewer line affects all of them at once. Symptoms of a main sewer line problem include multiple slow drains throughout the home, gurgling sounds from toilets when other fixtures are running, or sewer odor without an obvious source.
